    Measure the room

    1. Stand near the middle of the room.
    2. Hold the phone at chest height, an arm's length away.
    3. Ask everyone to stay quiet for 5 seconds.

    A 6-second tone rising from 40 Hz to 16 kHz. It measures every band, bass included, with far more signal than a clap, and gives the room's impulse response and frequency response.

      Reverberation time per band

      RT60 in each octave band. The green band is the target range for your use.

      Decay curves

      Energy left in the room after the sound, per octave band (Schroeder integration). A steeper line means a shorter RT60; a bend means two decay rates, often a flutter echo or a coupled space.

      Spectrogram of the decay

      Each frequency dying away over time; brighter is louder. Long streaks at the bottom are room modes ringing on.

      Frequency response

      Level at the mic per frequency, smoothed to 1/6 octave. It includes the speaker, so read the shape: peaks and dips below the Schroeder frequency come from the room's modes.

      Room modes

      Frequencies the room's dimensions resonate at. Axial modes (between two opposite walls) are the strongest. Below the Schroeder frequency they are far enough apart to be heard as boomy notes.
